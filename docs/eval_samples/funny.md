Pig in Pointe Shoes

Pim the pig lived on a muddy farm. Every spare minute, Pim twirled. She twirled in the puddles. Splish! Splash! Spin!

"Plié, plop, and a twirl for the top!" Pim sang, and down she went into the mud.

Her barnyard friends cheered. Clucky the hen flapped her wings. Dot the duck quacked a happy tune. Old Gus the goat banged a pail like a drum.

"One day," Pim said, "I will dance on the big stage in the village. I will wear pointe shoes and a tutu."

"A tutu?" asked Clucky. "What is a tutu?"

"It is a fluffy, twirly skirt," said Pim. "And I am going to have one."

So Pim sewed. She stitched a tutu from a flour sack and two old curtains. Snip, snip! Stitch, stitch! It was a little lumpy. It was a little bit itchy. But it was a tutu, and it swished when she walked.

Pim trotted down the road to the village dance school. Her tutu went swish, swish, swish.

At the door stood Madame Goose, tall and proud, with a neck as long as a garden hose.

"Goodness gracious!" Madame Goose gasped. "A pig in a flour-sack tutu!"

She unrolled a very long scroll. It went down the steps and all the way to the gate.

"Rule number one," Madame Goose read. "No mud on the floor. Rule number two. No flour sacks. Rule number three..."

Pim's ears drooped. Her curly tail drooped too. Maybe she would not be allowed in at all.

But Madame Goose was still reading. "Rule number forty-seven. Everyone who wants to dance must try."

Pim's ears popped right back up.

Inside the studio, the class began. Pim leapt across the floor. Whoosh! She flew so high that the hens in the corner flew up in a panic. Flap! Flap! Cluck! Feathers floated everywhere.

Next, Pim tried a pirouette. She spun and spun and spun, until she was spinning so fast that she knocked over the water buckets. Splash! Splosh! Water rolled all the way to the door.

The whole class giggled. Pim giggled too.

"Plié, plop, and a twirl for the top!" she said, and she tried again.

Madame Goose watched with her long neck bent low. "I have never seen anyone try so hard," she said quietly. And for the first time that day, she smiled.

The next week, the village picnic needed a show. The star dancer was Henrietta the hen, who had flapped her wings all morning. Now her wings were too tired to dance.

"Oh no," said Madame Goose. "Who will dance instead?"

Pim's heart went thump, thump, thump. "I will," she said. "But I cannot dance alone. Let's bring the whole barnyard!"

So they did. The cows waltzed in a slow, swishy circle. Moo, moo, one, two, three! The ducks lined up and pirouetted, one after another. Quack, quack, spin! And old Gus the goat leapt over the hay bales, over the picnic blankets, and right over the lemonade.

Pim led the way, with her tutu swirling and her hooves tapping.

"Plié, plop, and a twirl for the top!" she called, and everyone twirled into the big finale. The crowd clapped. The picnic baskets wobbled. One pie went flying, but nobody minded.

When the music stopped, everyone bowed. Pim bowed so low that her nose touched the grass.

Now the applause had faded. The sun had slipped behind the hills.

The animals walked home slowly, in a long, soft line. They curled up together in a big pile of warm hay.

Pim gave a huge, slow yawn. Then she gave another, softer one.

The crickets began to hum their little lullaby. The stars came out, one by one, like tiny lamps.

Pim's tutu rested on the hay. Her eyes grew heavy. Tomorrow, she thought, she would dance again.

And then her yawns came slower and softer, until her eyes closed, and she slept.

# Penelope Porkington Pirouettes Her Way to Glory

Penelope Porkington was a pig with a curly tail, a round belly, and a dream as big as a hay bale. She wanted to be a ballet dancer.

"Pigs can be anything!" she announced one morning, standing on a overturned bucket in the barnyard. "Even *prima ballerinas!*"

The goat, Gerald, chewed thoughtfully on a fence post. "Pigs are not built for ballet," he said. "You are built for rolling in mud."

"Rolling in mud *is* a dance move," Penelope sniffed. "It's called the Mud Swirl. Very advanced."

The hens clucked in disapproval. The cat yawned and went back to sleep. Nobody believed in Penelope except Penelope herself.

So she practiced. Every dawn she rose before the rooster and attempted a *plié* in the puddle by the water trough. She stretched her hind legs until they wobbled like jelly. She twirled until she was so dizzy she fell into the duck pond, and the ducks had to rescue her with a stick.

"Again!" she gasped, dripping with pond weed.

After three months of relentless practice, Penelope marched into town wearing a pink tutu she had sewn from a bedsheet. It was a little itchy, and it smelled faintly of hay, but she had never felt more graceful.

The Grand Ballet Company was holding auditions in the Opera House. Penelope waddled up the red carpet, her curly tail bouncing with every step.

The judges were three very serious swans, a stern-looking heron named Madame Flounce, and a tuba player who had fallen asleep.

"Name?" asked Madame Flounce.

"Penelope Porkington!" she squealed. "And I shall now perform *Swan Lake!*"

"Swine Lake," corrected Penelope, quickly. "It's a more *pig-friendly* version."

She leapt onto the stage. She spun. She arched her back in a graceful *arabesque*, wobbled dangerously, and then, in a moment of pure inspiration, flung herself into a magnificent *grand jeté*.

Unfortunately, the stage had just been mopped. Penelope slid across the floor on her belly like a hockey puck, shot off the edge, and landed with a tremendous *SPLOOSH* directly into the orchestra pit, right inside the tuba.

*BWAAAAAAAAAMP!*

The tuba player woke up with a start. The audience gasped. The swans dropped their clipboards.

Then, from deep inside the tuba, came a muffled voice: "Did I do the Mud Swirl? I think I did the Mud Swirl!"

There was a long silence. Then Madame Flounce began to laugh. Then the swans began to laugh. Soon the whole theater was roaring with laughter, and the tuba player was giggling so hard he had to put down his instrument.

Penelope climbed out, dripping and glittery with confetti, and bowed deeply.

"Ladies and gentlemen," she announced, "the Mud Swirl!"

She twirled, she flopped, she rolled, and she wiggled her curly tail with such joy that even the stern heron tapped her foot.

When she finished, the audience leapt to their feet, hooting and cheering.

Madame Flounce cleared her throat. "Miss Porkington, we have never seen anything quite like that. Would you like to join the company?"

Penelope squealed so loudly that every window in the Opera House rattled.

And that is how the first pig ever to dance with the Grand Ballet Company became a star. She performed in *Swine Lake* every Saturday night, and she always finished with a grand, muddy finale.

Gerald the goat still says pigs are not built for ballet.

But Penelope just wiggles her tail, grins, and says, "Gerald, you've never seen me *pirouette* in a bathtub full of pudding."
