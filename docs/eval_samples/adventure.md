Pickle and the Path to the Moon

Pickle was a brave little hedgehog. Pickle lived in a cozy hollow under an old oak tree.

Pickle loved daytime adventures. Pickle loved rolling through the clover and sniffing the warm grass. But when the sun went down, Pickle's tummy went flutter, flutter, flutter. Pickle was scared of the dark.

One evening, Pickle poked a nose out of the hollow. Up over the hill, something silver was peeking out. "Oh my," whispered Pickle. "It's the moon!"

Pickle thought and thought. "I will go find the moon," said Pickle. "Step by step, up to the moon!"

Pickle packed a small satchel. In went a lantern, a biscuit, a ball of string, and an empty jar. Then Pickle set off along a silver path through the meadow.

Pitter-patter went Pickle's feet. Hoot, hoot! called an owl from a branch. "Where are you going, little hedgehog?"

"To find the moon," said Pickle. "Step by step, up to the moon!"

"Hoo-hoo! Good luck!" said the owl. Pickle broke the biscuit in half and shared it with the owl. "Yum, thank you!" hooted the owl.

Glowing mushrooms blinked along the path, blue and green. Fireflies zipped around Pickle's head, blink, blink, blink. Pickle giggled. "The dark is not so scary with friends," said Pickle.

Pickle came to a tall pine tree. The path grew dark under the branches, so Pickle lit the lantern. Its warm glow made a golden circle on the ground. "I will climb to the moon!" Pickle climbed and climbed. Ouch! The pine needles were prickly. Pickle slid right back down. "Too prickly," said Pickle, with a little huff. "Step by step, up to the moon!"

Next, Pickle found a pond. Pickle picked a big leaf and hopped on. "I will float to the moon!" But a breeze blew, and the leaf drifted away, away, away. Pickle bobbed on the water. "Oh dear," said Pickle. "That didn't work either."

Pickle went back to the owl. "Wise owl, how do I reach the moon?"

The owl blinked slowly. "Hoo. The moon is best reached by going the way the river goes."

Pickle smiled. "Step by step, up to the moon!"

A friendly river came chuckling along. "Hello, Pickle! I can carry you partway," it said. But the water swirled and bubbled.

Pickle's tummy fluttered. Then Pickle remembered the owl's words. Pickle tied one end of the string to a reed on the bank. "This will be our safe line," said Pickle. "Fireflies, will you light the way with me?"

The fireflies gathered in a glowing cloud. Blink, blink, blink! Together they waded across the river, one careful step at a time, with Pickle holding the string and the fireflies lighting the way.

On the far shore, a gentle green hill rose toward the sky. Pickle climbed it, step by step, past the soft clover. At the very top, the moon hung so low and so close that its silver light spilled over the grass like a soft white blanket. Pickle stretched up on tiptoe and reached out. A sparkling ripple of moonlight wiggled right in front of Pickle's nose. Gently, Pickle scooped it into the empty jar with both paws. It glowed warm and bright.

Pickle did not feel scared at all. Pickle felt proud. And Pickle felt calm, like a warm blanket.

Pickle and the fireflies waded back across the river, holding the string tight. Then Pickle walked home along the silver path, carrying the moonlight in the jar. The lantern swung gently from one paw, its warm glow lighting the way.

"Here, Owl," said Pickle. The owl hooted softly and fluffed its feathers. "Here, Fireflies," said Pickle. The fireflies twinkled a happy thank-you.

The night felt friendly now. It felt like home.

Back in the hollow, Pickle hung the lantern on a hook and set the jar beside the bed. The moonlight glowed softly, like a night-light.

The meadow hummed quietly. The fireflies dimmed, one by one. The owl settled into a branch.

Pickle snuggled into a soft bed of leaves. The moon kept watch.

Pickle's eyes grew heavy. Pickle drifted into a sleepy, contented dream.

# Pickle and the Moon

Pickle was a hedgehog with a very prickly coat and a very soft heart. Every evening, when the sun slipped behind the hills and the sky turned the color of blackberry jam, Pickle curled into the smallest ball he could manage and hid under his blanket of dry leaves.

"The dark is too big," he whispered to himself. "It has too many shadows, and shadows have too many secrets."

One night, Pickle peeked out from his leaves and saw something glowing through the gap in the willow tree. It was round and silver and soft, like a dandelion puff that had caught the light.

"What's that?" Pickle squeaked.

His best friend, Mrs. Thimbleweed the old tortoise, was slowly making her way across the garden. "That, little one, is the Moon. She lights the night so that no one has to be lost in it."

Pickle's eyes grew wide. "Has she ever been close? Could I visit her?"

Mrs. Thimbleweed chuckled. "The Moon lives very, very far away, Pickle. But if you want to see her up close, you'll have to be brave enough to walk through the dark."

Pickle's quills trembled. But something in his chest felt warm and determined, like a tiny ember.

"I'll go," he said. "Tonight."

---

Pickle packed his supplies carefully: one acorn for a snack, one smooth pebble for luck, and a small bit of string he'd found under the garden bench. Then he tucked his blanket around his shoulders and stepped out past the hedge.

The garden felt different at night. The roses had gone gray and quiet. The birdbath looked like a sleeping ghost. Every rustle made Pickle jump.

*Rustle. Rustle.*

"Who's there?" Pickle called, his voice tiny.

A pair of round yellow eyes blinked from a branch above. "Only me," said Owl. "Whooo's out so late?"

"I'm Pickle. I'm going to find the Moon."

Owl tilted his head. "The Moon is a long way off, you know. Are you sure you're not too scared?"

Pickle swallowed hard. "I'm scared," he admitted. "But I'm going anyway."

Owl blinked slowly, then smiled. "That's the bravest kind of going. Follow the stream. It runs toward the hill, and the hill is the nearest place to the sky."

---

Pickle followed the stream, his little feet splashing through cool water. Fireflies blinked on and off around him like tiny lanterns, and a frog croaked a sleepy song from the reeds.

Soon the path grew narrow and the trees grew tall. Their branches stretched above him like long, grasping fingers.

*Creak. Creak.*

Pickle froze. His heart thumped like a drum. He wanted nothing more than to turn around and run home to his leaves.

Then he looked down at the smooth pebble in his paw. He remembered Owl's words, and Mrs. Thimbleweed's gentle voice.

"Being brave doesn't mean you aren't scared," Pickle whispered. "It means you keep going anyway."

So he took one step. Then another. And another.

At the top of the hill, the trees parted, and Pickle gasped.

The sky was enormous. It was full of tiny, twinkling stars, and in the middle of it all hung the Moon, round and silver and glowing. She looked down at him with a kind, gentle face, as if she had been waiting for him all along.

"Hello," Pickle breathed.

The Moon's light spilled over the hilltop and onto his prickles, turning each one into a shining silver spike. The shadows around him didn't look so scary anymore. They looked like soft gray blankets, resting on the grass.

"You're not so big after all," Pickle said to the dark. "You're just quiet."

---

Pickle sat on the hilltop for a long time, eating his acorn and watching the stars. When he finally began the walk home, the path didn't feel nearly as long. The fireflies guided him, the frog cheered him on, and Owl gave him a wink from the willow tree.

When Pickle crawled back under his blanket of leaves, he yawned a huge, sleepy yawn.

"Did you find the Moon?" asked Mrs. Thimbleweed, who had waited up for him in her cozy shell.

"I did," said Pickle, smiling. "She's wonderful. And the dark isn't so scary once you walk right through it."

From then on, whenever Pickle felt a little afraid, he would look up at the silver Moon and remember his adventure. And he would tuck his blanket around his shoulders, take a deep breath, and go out to meet the night.

*The End.*
